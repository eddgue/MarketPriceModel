Component guide
Market reference

The series is the USDA National Shipping Point Trends weekly print, report code FVWTRDS, My Market News report 1662: Roma plum type, 25 lb cartons loose, extra large, Mexico crossings through Texas, with Nogales as the fallback district when Texas is not quoted. Each weekly value is the midpoint of the printed "mostly" range. Single-value prints stand as printed. The issue carries Monday spot prices and releases Tuesday, slipping a day on federal holidays. A print is the published observation itself, the quote USDA puts on the wire for that date; mid-mostly is the calculation this model applies to it.

Normalization band

The corridor around the active base reference, set as a percentage in either direction. A weekly print outside the band starts the trigger clock. Two consecutive weeks outside confirm an interim adjustment, and two consecutive weeks back inside confirm reversion to base. Tight bands trigger on noise. Wide bands sit silent through real moves. A working calibration is about twice the reference's average absolute weekly move.

Reset every

How often the base reference and base price recalculate on schedule, regardless of interim activity in the prior cycle. Shorter cadence keeps the base honest at the cost of admin churn. Longer cadence stabilizes cost and raises the odds a reset lands mid-spike, locking a distorted base in place for a full cycle. The March 10 reset in this window does exactly that.

Reset trailing

The lookback window that sets each new base, averaged over the most recent reported weeks. Longer windows smooth single-print noise but drag stale levels into the new base. A setting of 1 marks the base to the last single print.

Interim trailing

The lookback that prices an interim adjustment once a trigger confirms. Short windows track the exact move that fired the trigger. Longer windows blend in pre-move weeks and under-correct in fast markets. The default of 2 uses the two confirming prints.

Trigger rights

Automatic, bilateral fires the interim whenever the condition confirms, in either direction, for either party. Kroger optional reproduces the draft contract language: adjustments fire only when they lower the buyer's price, leaving the supplier holding upside moves until the next scheduled reset. Toggling between the two prices that option against real market data.

Discount form

The awarded bid, taken off the reference as fixed dollars per carton or as a percentage. Fixed dollars keep bids comparable across market levels and keep supplier margin legible in cents. A percentage scales the discount with the market, which pushes suppliers to price spike insurance into the bid.

Re-mark

With re-mark on, an active interim re-prices when the market breaches the band measured against the interim reference for two consecutive weeks. Off reproduces the draft: a frozen interim that a second-leg move runs away from, recreating the original lag one tier up.

Confirmation and lag

Trigger confirm sets how many consecutive reported weeks outside the band confirm an interim adjustment, and the same count governs re-marks. Revert confirm sets the consecutive weeks back inside that end one. Short confirmation tracks the market tightly and churns on noise. Long confirmation filters noise and lags real moves, shifting cost to whoever holds the lag. Keep interim trailing at or below trigger confirm so the interim prices off breach weeks rather than the calm before them. Unequal entry and exit create drift toward one party, so set asymmetry deliberately or keep them equal. Effectivity stays fixed at one week after confirmation.

Reading the chart

The gray dashed line is the weekly market print. Dots mark verified USDA weeks; segments between dots are linear fills pending the complete series. The stepped line is the contract price: green while on base, red while on an interim adjustment. The shaded corridor is the normalization band around the active base, and dashed verticals mark scheduled resets. Red triangles mark the week a trigger takes effect, red diamonds mark re-marks, and green down-triangles mark reversion to base. Confirmations overridden by a same-week scheduled reset never price an invoice, so they draw no marker and do not count.

Daily overlay

USDA also publishes daily shipping point prices for the same spec in the National FOB Review and the Tomato Fax Report. The overlay draws those daily mid-mostly values as a thin line behind the weekly series to show real intra-week fluctuation. It is context only: the band, confirmation counts and resets all run on the weekly print, because a daily clock would churn adjustments and invite gaming of single prints.

Metric cards

Averages run over the full window at one carton per week. Gap vs pass-through compares contract cost to market less the awarded discount: negative means the contract ran under spot net, positive means it ran over.

Spend over range

Cases bought weekly scales the model to dollars. From and To scope the calculation to any stretch of the window. Positive is savings against market less discount, negative is premium, and the rows beneath split the result by base period, so each reset cycle answers for its own economics at the base it carried.

Semi-live data

USDA MARS API is free with registration. The companion script usda_refresh.py pulls each configured series, computes mid-mostly on the locked spec, and writes usda_data.js next to this file; the app loads it automatically on open. Browsers block a local file from calling the API directly, so the script does the fetching. My Market News holds the full shipping point history, which covers multi-year band calibration per commodity. Run the script each Tuesday after the issue posts and the evaluation stays at most a week behind the market.
